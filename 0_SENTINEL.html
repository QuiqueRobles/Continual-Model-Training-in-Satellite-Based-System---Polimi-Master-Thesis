Sentinel-2

How the Satellite Works for Flood Detection

Everything you need to know about Sentinel-2 for this WorldFloods v2 project — focused on the 6 spectral bands used by our UNet model, plus the Myriad hardware used in onboard AI applications.

🌍 What is Sentinel-2?

Sentinel-2 is part of the European Copernicus Programme, operated by the European Space Agency (ESA). It consists of a constellation of satellites that provide high-resolution optical imagery of the Earth’s land and coastal areas.

The mission is designed for operational monitoring of agriculture, forests, land-use change, coastal zones, and — crucially for this project — flood events and surface water mapping.

📡 Mission Specifications (Relevant for Our Project)

  • Satellites: Sentinel-2A (launched 2015), Sentinel-2B (2017), and Sentinel-2C (operational since 2024)
  • Revisit time: 5 days at the equator (with two satellites), even faster at higher latitudes
  • Swath width: 290 km — allows wide-area coverage in a single pass
  • Data products used in WorldFloods v2: Level-1C (Top-of-Atmosphere reflectance) — the exact product our model was trained on

Why this matters for flood mapping: The frequent revisit time means we can capture flood events quickly, even if clouds are present in some images.

🔬 The Multispectral Instrument (MSI)

Each Sentinel-2 satellite carries the Multispectral Instrument (MSI), which captures reflected sunlight in 13 spectral bands ranging from visible to shortwave infrared.

The bands have three different spatial resolutions:
  • 4 bands at 10 m
  • 6 bands at 20 m
  • 3 bands at 60 m
In our project, the model works with tiles of 256×256 pixels at 10 m resolution (after resampling the 20 m bands).

🎯 The 6 Bands Used in This Project (“bgriswirs” configuration)

Our UNet model was trained on exactly these six bands:

Band Name Central Wavelength Bandwidth Native Resolution Why it’s useful for flood / water detection
B2 Blue 492–493 nm 65 nm 10 m Good water penetration; helps distinguish clear vs. turbid water and provides true-color context.
B3 Green 559–560 nm 35–36 nm 10 m Excellent contrast between clear and muddy water; used in NDWI water index.
B4 Red 664–665 nm 30–31 nm 10 m Strong absorption by water; high contrast with vegetation and land.
B8 Near Infrared (NIR) 832–833 nm 105–106 nm 10 m Key water detector: Water absorbs NIR almost completely (very low reflectance), while land and vegetation reflect it strongly.
B11 Shortwave Infrared 1 (SWIR1) 1610 nm ~90–100 nm 20 m (resampled) Extremely sensitive to moisture; water appears almost black. Excellent at separating water from clouds and shadows.
B12 Shortwave Infrared 2 (SWIR2) 2190 nm ~180 nm 20 m (resampled) Best cloud vs. water discriminator: Water absorbs very strongly; clouds and snow reflect more in this region.

Why these exact 6 bands? They combine the best visible/NIR water-contrast bands with the two SWIR bands that dramatically improve cloud masking and water detection accuracy. This combination is known in the ml4floods library as the “bgriswirs” configuration.

💧 How Sentinel-2 “Sees” Water vs. Clouds vs. Land

  • Water: Very low reflectance in NIR (B8) and especially in SWIR (B11/B12). It appears dark in these bands.
  • Land / vegetation: High reflectance in NIR (B8), moderate in visible bands.
  • Clouds: High reflectance across visible and NIR bands, but different behavior in SWIR. This is why B11 and B12 are critical for the model to avoid confusing clouds with water.

Our model learns these spectral signatures directly from the 6-band input, which is why it can output separate water and cloud probability maps.

⚡ The Myriad Part That Uses Sentinel-2 Data (Onboard AI)

The Intel Movidius Myriad (specifically Myriad 2 and Myriad X) is a low-power Vision Processing Unit (VPU) designed for efficient computer vision and deep neural network inference at the edge.

Key characteristics of the Myriad hardware:
  • Extremely low power consumption (typically 1–2 Watts)
  • High performance for CNN inference (hundreds of GOPs to TOPs depending on version)
  • Optimized for embedded and space-constrained environments
  • Supports many common deep learning layers used in segmentation models like UNet

How it connects to Sentinel-2:

While your current WorldFloods v2 evaluation script runs on the ground using pre-processed Level-1C Sentinel-2 images, the Myriad VPU enables **onboard processing** directly on the satellite.

The most famous example is the **Φsat-1** mission (part of the European Space Agency’s Φsat series), which was the first satellite to run a convolutional neural network (CNN) in orbit for cloud detection. It used the **Myriad 2 VPU** to process multispectral imagery (similar to Sentinel-2 bands) in real time and discard cloudy images before downlink — dramatically reducing data volume sent to Earth.

Relevance to flood detection and your project:

  • Future missions could run your UNet-style water detection model directly onboard using a Myriad-class processor.
  • This would allow real-time flood mapping, immediate alerts, and smart data selection (e.g., only downlink clear-water scenes).
  • The same 6-band “bgriswirs” input your model uses today could be fed into a quantized version of the network running on the Myriad VPU.
  • Myriad hardware has been radiation-tested and space-qualified for Low Earth Orbit (LEO), making it suitable for small satellites and CubeSats.

Why Myriad matters for the future of Sentinel-2-like missions: Traditional satellites downlink huge amounts of raw or lightly processed data. Onboard AI with Myriad reduces bandwidth needs, enables faster response times for disasters (like floods), and makes small, affordable satellites much more capable.

📦 Data Level Used in This Project

The WorldFloods v2 dataset (and therefore our model) uses Sentinel-2 Level-1C products:

• Top-of-Atmosphere (TOA) reflectance
• Already orthorectified and geometrically corrected
• No atmospheric correction applied (Level-2A would be Bottom-of-Atmosphere)

Using L1C is a deliberate choice in flood mapping because it allows faster processing and the model was trained to handle atmospheric effects implicitly.

📌 Why This Matters for Our Project

The combination of high spatial resolution (10 m), frequent revisit, and rich spectral information (especially the NIR + SWIR bands) makes Sentinel-2 one of the best satellites for operational flood monitoring. Our UNet model was specifically designed around these six bands to maximize water detection accuracy while correctly handling clouds — the two biggest challenges in optical flood mapping.

The Myriad VPU opens the door to running similar models **onboard**, bringing us closer to real-time, intelligent satellite systems.
